Disability

If you mentioned a disability on your CV, you might want to say more about it in your cover letter. Organisations like Disability UK can give you advice. You do not have to mention it at this stage if you prefer not to.

Ending your cover letter

Finish politely and make it easy to reach you.

1

Thank the employer for considering your application.

2

Tell them they can get more details from your CV and that you are looking forward to hearing from them.

3

Say how they can contact you. Make sure the details are correct on both your letter and your CV.

Tips for writing a cover letter

Nine things to remember every time.

1Write a new letter for every job and tailor it to the company and the specific role.
2Use the same font and size as your CV, so it looks consistent.
3Make sure the company name and the recruiter’s details are correct.
4Use the right language and tone: stay professional and match the keywords in the job advert.
5Show you have researched the job and the company.
6Highlight your most relevant skills and experience to stand out from other applicants.
7Back up statements with facts and use the STAR method.
8Double-check your spelling and grammar before you send it.
9Keep a copy of your letter. They may ask you about it in an interview.

The STAR method

A simple way to back up your statements with facts.

S

Situation

Describe the situation you were in.

T

Task

Explain the task you had to do.

A

Action

Say what action you took.

R

Result

Share the result you achieved.

Example SOur shop’s sales were falling. TI had to win back customers. AI launched a social-media campaign. RSales rose by 20% in three months.

Putting it together

The structure of a cover letter

3 to 5 paragraphs
GreetingAddress the letter to a named person. Check their name, title, company and postcode.
1. IntroductionSay who you are, which job you want and where you found it. Add the reference number.
2. Why youMatch your skills and experience to what the employer needs. Use facts and the STAR method.
3. Why themShow your research and that you share their values, culture and style. Add extra information if it helps.
4. ClosingThank them, point to your CV, give your contact details and say you look forward to hearing from them.

Key vocabulary

Useful words from the text.

vacancy

noun

a job that is available for someone to do

…not applying to a vacancy…

recruiter

noun

a person whose job is to find and choose new employees

…the recruiter’s details are correct

tailor

verb

to make something suit one particular person or purpose

…tailored to the company and the specific role

keen

adjective

very interested and eager to do something

…let the employer know you’re keen

reference number

noun

a code that identifies one particular job advert

…reference number if there is one

postcode

noun (UK)

letters and numbers in an address that help the post reach it

…address, including postcode

employment gap

phrase

a period of time when you were not working

…gaps in your employment history

tone

noun

the attitude your writing shows, for example professional

…the right language and tone
